### Where Demand Factor Errors Start

Most redesigns trace back to a borrowed spreadsheet. A load schedule built for a 2015 mid-income project gets copied into a premium tower with split ACs in every bedroom, induction cooking and EV-ready parking, and nobody revisits the demand factors. Connected load looks similar on paper. Coincident load does not. A demand factor that suited an older apartment can understate the evening peak of a modern 3 BHK by a wide margin, and that gap multiplies across four hundred flats.

Sound substation planning starts by separating connected load from probable maximum demand, then testing that figure against how residents behave in June rather than an average day in March. Noida’s summer evenings, when every compressor runs together, are the real design condition. Everything else is a comfortable day.

### Diversity Across a Tower Cluster

A single tower and a cluster of six behave differently. Diversity between towers is real, but smaller than most schedules assume, because the same weather, working hours, and school calendar synchronise the occupants. Seasoned [**Electrical Design Consultants Noida**](https://sanelac.com/service/electrical-design-services/) treat inter-tower diversity as something to earn with data, never to grant by default. They apply conservative values to residential blocks, separate factors to retail podiums and clubhouses whose peaks fall at different hours, and keep a documented reason beside every number. When a reviewer or the distribution licensee asks why a figure was chosen, the answer is already on the sheet.

### Common-Area Loads That Get Shortchanged

Apartment loads draw the attention, yet the common-area side quietly decides transformer size. Three items cause most of the trouble:

- **Lifts:** a bank of eight high-speed cars has starting currents and regenerative drives that behave differently from nameplate kW, and they shape harmonic content on the LT bus.
- **Fire and life safety:** pressurisation fans, jockey and main pumps and smoke extraction must stay live on DG during an emergency, which sets generator rating independently of normal demand.
- **EV charging:** even at modest uptake today, spare ways, cable routes and transformer headroom cost little at design stage and a fortune after handover.

Good **substation planning** keeps these loads in separate categories, because essential, standby and normal supplies are sized against different failure scenarios.

### Substation Room Decisions That Cannot Be Undone

Concrete does not negotiate. Transformer count, ratings, RMU position, LT panel room, and cable trench depth are fixed once the basement slab is cast. Two 1600 kVA units on an N+1 philosophy leave a very different upgrade path from three 1000 kVA units, and the right choice depends on phasing as much as final load. If towers are handed over in stages, the electrical room must energise cleanly for tower one while leaving physical space for the rest. Phased substation planning must also respect the sanctioned load and the licensee’s metering and HT connection requirements, and since those requirements change, the approach deserves written confirmation before drawings freeze.

Heat rejection, flood level, DG exhaust routing and fire separation belong in the same conversation. That is why the better [**Electrical Design Consultants Noida**](https://sanelac.com/service/electrical-design-services/) coordinate early with structural and MEP teams instead of handing over a single-line diagram and walking away. A transformer room that is 600 mm short in length is not an electrical error on paper, but it becomes one in the field.

### Checks Before Drawings Freeze

Short-circuit level at the HT point, voltage drop in the rising mains at full-floor loading, harmonic distortion from lifts, VFDs and EV chargers, and earthing to IS 3043 principles should all be reviewed before issue for construction. Compliance with NBC 2016 and the CEA safety regulations is the floor, not the target. A peer review by someone who did not draw the sheet catches the assumptions the original designer can no longer see.

Reconcile the load letter sent to the licensee with the final load schedule as well. A mismatch between the two is one of the more common reasons energisation slips at handover.

### FAQs

**Q1.Does a wrong demand factor really force a redesign?**  
Yes. An understated demand factor usually surfaces as transformer overloading, undersized DG capacity, or a shortage of LT panel space after occupancy, and fixing those means civil changes, shutdowns, and re-approval. Correcting the figure on paper costs a few hours; correcting it in a commissioned basement costs months.

**Q2. Is it safe to apply diversity between towers?**  
Only with conservative, documented values. Towers in one cluster share weather, working hours, and school schedules, so their evening peaks overlap more than expected. Aggressive diversity saves transformer capacity on paper but risks summer overload, while modest, justified factors protect both budget and reliability.

**Q3. Should EV charging be included in today’s load calculation?**  
Yes, at least as provision. Reserve transformer headroom, spare breaker ways and cable routes now, and install the chargers in phases later. Retrofitting capacity into an occupied tower costs far more than leaving space during design, so provision works as cheap insurance.

**Q4. Can one transformer room serve a multi-tower cluster?**  
Often yes, but it depends on distance, voltage drop, and phasing. A central room reduces HT cabling yet lengthens LT runs, while distributed rooms shorten LT feeders. Sound **substation planning** compares both layouts on cost, losses, and maintenance access before committing.
